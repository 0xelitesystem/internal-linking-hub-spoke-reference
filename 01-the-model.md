# The model

In the hub-and-spoke model, a hub page covers a topic broadly and serves as the central entry point, while spoke pages each cover one subtopic in depth. The hub links out to every spoke, and every spoke links back to the hub, forming a connected cluster around the topic.

## The hub

The hub is a broad page on a topic that introduces it, summarizes its parts, and links out to the detailed pages on each subtopic. It is the central entry point for the topic, the page you want to rank for the broad term and the one that ties the cluster together. The hub does not have to cover everything in depth itself; its job is to map the topic and route readers to the depth on the spokes.

## The spokes

Each spoke is a detailed page on one subtopic of the hub's theme. Spokes go deep on their specific subject, which is where the substantive content lives. A spoke targets the more specific terms within the topic and serves the reader who wants depth on that particular piece. Collectively the spokes cover the topic thoroughly, with each handling its own part well rather than all of them shallowly.

## The connections

The defining feature is the linking pattern: the hub links to all its spokes, each spoke links back to the hub, and spokes link to related spokes where relevant. This creates a tightly connected cluster where every page is reachable from the hub and the relationships between pages are explicit. The connections are what turn a set of pages on related topics into a structured cluster that functions as a unit.

## The cluster as a unit

The result is a topic cluster: a hub and its spokes operating together as a coherent treatment of a topic. A site can have several such clusters, each centered on a hub. Thinking in clusters rather than isolated pages changes how you plan content: you build out a topic by creating a hub and its spokes together, structured and interlinked from the start, rather than publishing unconnected pages and hoping they cohere.
