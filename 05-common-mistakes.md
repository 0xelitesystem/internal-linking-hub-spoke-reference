# Common mistakes

The common hub-and-spoke mistakes are orphaned pages with no links in, a hub that only links out without spokes linking back, overlapping spokes that compete with each other, vague anchor text, and treating internal linking as an afterthought rather than a planned structure.

## Orphaned pages

An orphaned page has no internal links pointing to it, so it is hard for readers and engines to discover and it sits outside the cluster's authority flow. Orphans are a common waste: good content that no one finds because nothing links to it. Every page should be linked into the cluster, the spokes from the hub and related spokes, so none is stranded. An orphaned spoke is a spoke that does not function as part of the cluster.

## One-way linking

A hub that links out to spokes while the spokes never link back breaks the model's authority concentration. The reciprocal link, spoke back to hub, is what builds up the hub and keeps the cluster connected. Linking only one direction leaves the structure incomplete and the authority flow lopsided. Both directions are required for the cluster to do what it is meant to do; a one-way version is a partial structure that loses much of the benefit.

## Overlapping spokes

When two spokes cover the same subtopic, they compete with each other for the same terms and split the authority that should concentrate on one page. Overlapping spokes are a planning failure: the cluster's subtopics should be distinct, each spoke owning its own piece. Before creating a spoke, confirm it covers ground no existing spoke does. Distinct, non-overlapping spokes are what let each rank for its own terms instead of cannibalizing a sibling.

## Treating it as an afterthought

The largest mistake is not planning the structure at all, publishing pages as they come and adding links haphazardly later. The model's benefits come from deliberate structure: a planned hub, planned spokes, and intentional linking. Retrofitting structure onto a pile of unconnected pages is harder and less effective than building it in from the start. Internal linking works as a strategy when it is planned as one, and underperforms when treated as cleanup after the fact.
